TAKING ACTION

FIGHTING MISINFORMATION

Practical ways individuals can make better decisions online.

HOW CAN INDIVIDUALS HELP?

01. Check the source

  • Identify who created the information.
  • Check whether the author or organization is credible.
  • Look for an original source instead of relying on a repost.
  • Be cautious of websites pretending to be established news organizations.

02. Verify the information

  • Compare the claim with multiple reliable sources.
  • Look for evidence, statistics, and expert explanations.
  • Check whether fact-checking organizations have investigated the claim.
  • Do not assume a claim is accurate just because many people shared it.

03. Read beyond the headline

  • Read the full article before forming an opinion.
  • Check whether the headline accurately represents the article.
  • Look for missing details or important context.
  • Be cautious of headlines designed mainly to attract attention.

04. Examine images and videos

  • Check where an image or video originally appeared.
  • Look for signs that media has been edited or taken out of context.
  • Compare the media with reliable reporting about the same event.
  • Use reverse image searching when the original context is unclear.

05. Think before sharing

  • Pause before sharing surprising or emotional content.
  • Ask whether the information has been verified.
  • Avoid spreading claims that cannot be supported by evidence.
  • Correct inaccurate information respectfully when reliable evidence is available.

06. Report misleading content

  • Use a platform's reporting tools when content violates its rules.
  • Provide context when reporting misleading posts.
  • Avoid reposting the content just to criticize it, since this can spread it further.
